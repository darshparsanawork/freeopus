What this model actually does: it never sees the video or audio. It only reads the text transcript (with timestamps) plus the scene-cut list, and decides which moments are worth turning into a clip — for each one, it writes a start/end time, a title, a one-line reason, and a 0-100 virality score. Downloading, transcribing, cropping, and captioning all happen separately in plain code, regardless of which model you pick here. Because it's a pure text-reasoning task (not vision, not long-context video), you don't need the most expensive model — a fast/cheap "flash"-tier model is usually enough; a stronger model mainly buys you punchier titles and pickier moment selection.

What the original OpenShorts project uses: per its own docs, OpenShorts runs this exact step on Google Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite. Good starting points here: any gemini-*-flash model (fast, cheap, close to what OpenShorts itself uses) for speed/cost, or claude-*-sonnet / gpt-4o-class models on OpenRouter if you want more careful moment picking and don't mind paying more per video.
Transcription turns the video's audio into a word-by-word text transcript (with timestamps) — this is what powers subtitles/captions and is what the LLM above reads to pick moments. It's a separate step from the LLM and runs independently of it.
